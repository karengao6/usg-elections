USG Elections

Who do you want to lead USG?
Here’s a guide to the candidates.

November 2026

Nassau Hall/Photo courtesy of Emily Tang (The Daily Princetonian)

10 min read By the 'Prince' ed board

The USG elections each Winter are a unique opportunity to influence major undergraduate positions on issues ranging from sustainability to undergraduate mental health. The Editorial Board of The Daily Princetonian, a seven-member body of ‘Prince’ staffers and editors that serves as an institutional voice of the newspaper, conducted extensive research and deliberation over the course of the past week to determine the best candidates.

Some candidates impressed us, while others’ policies did not match with their lofty goals. In general, we would have liked to see a more competitive field down the ballot, and we hope that next year’s races will all be rigorously contested.

Each candidate's statement is linked in their expanded profile.

USG Academics Chair

Endorsed
Not Endorsed

Editorial note

The Editorial Board will not endorse a candidate for the Academics Chair.


Current USG members spoke to us about the importance of an active and assertive leader as Academics Chair, who is persistent in their communication with administrators. This is a role with considerable influence, especially with controversial issues like the ChatGPT pilot, proctoring, and changes to the Writing Seminar program. A contentious race would indicate student interest in academic policy, and pressure candidates to be specific and innovative in their platforms. We hope that more students run in the next election cycle.

USG Undergraduate Student Life Committee Chair

Endorsed
Not Endorsed

Editorial note

The Editorial Board will not endorse either candidate for Undergraduate Student Life Committee Chair. We were underwhelmed by both candidates’ platforms and previous track record in USG. In a role that is uniquely co-chaired by the University’s VP for Student Life, the USLC plays an important role that balances access to University leadership with student life. Yet, neither candidate provided a sufficient answer as to how they would best utilize this position.
